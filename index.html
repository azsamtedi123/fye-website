How it works

From a real challenge to a real solution.

Instead of learning about the energy transition only in theory, students work on challenges that organisations actually have. Lecturers coach. Professionals share what they know from practice.

  1. An organisation brings a challenge

    A real question from the energy transition, with real constraints and stakeholders.

  2. A mixed team picks it up

    MBO, HBO and WO students from different study programmes work on it together.

  3. Lecturers coach the process

    Less preparing classes, more helping students find out what they need to know.

  4. Ideas, prototypes and people

    The organisation gets fresh perspectives and gets to know possible future colleagues.

About us · Our story

Creating the energy future together.

The energy transition is one of the biggest challenges of our time. No single person, organisation or field of knowledge can solve it alone. That is where Future (y)our Energy comes in.

Future (y)our Energy (FyE) is a public private partnership connected to HAN. It is a collaborative space where students, lecturers, researchers, professionals and organisations come together to work on real challenges within the energy transition.

Organisations bring real-life challenges to FyE. People from different backgrounds and disciplines explore them together. The goal is not always to immediately find one perfect answer. Sometimes the first step is to understand the problem better, question existing ways of working and discover possibilities nobody had considered.

FyE is also a place where learning happens through experience. Students develop their knowledge, skills and confidence while working alongside lecturers, researchers and professionals.

Our mission

Connect education with practice.

Future (y)our Energy connects students, teachers, educational institutions and organisations to work together on real challenges within the energy transition. FyE brings together different study programmes, educational levels and areas of expertise, creating opportunities for people to learn from each other and approach complex energy challenges from different perspectives.

Our mission is to connect education with practice and create meaningful opportunities for students to apply their knowledge outside the classroom. Through real projects and challenges provided by organisations, students gain practical experience, develop new skills and learn how to work together across disciplines. At the same time, organisations share their knowledge and challenges, receive fresh perspectives from students and connect with potential future talent.

By creating these connections, FyE supports knowledge sharing between education and organisations and helps develop new ways of working and thinking about the energy transition.

Our vision

Collaboration as a natural part of learning and innovation.

Future (y)our Energy envisions a future where education and organisations work closely together to respond to the challenges of the energy transition. The transition requires more than knowledge within individual study programmes or organisations. It requires people from different backgrounds to work together, share knowledge and develop solutions based on real challenges.

Students should have opportunities to work on relevant challenges, gain experience with different disciplines and understand how their knowledge can contribute to society. Organisations should be able to work with education as a valuable partner, bringing real challenges into learning while connecting with new ideas, knowledge and future professionals.

FyE wants to strengthen these connections across institutions and disciplines, and help prepare students and organisations for a changing energy sector: a smarter, more collaborative and sustainable energy future.

Why FyE?

Because some challenges need us to work together.

  • The energy transition needs people who are willing to look beyond the usual way of doing things.
  • Students should have the opportunity to work on challenges that have a purpose.
  • Organisations can benefit from fresh perspectives and new knowledge.
  • Lecturers and researchers can share their expertise while learning from others.
  • Some of the biggest challenges of our future require us to work together instead of separately.

Case study · Alliander

How do you make essential energy infrastructure welcome in a neighbourhood?

PartnerAlliander
LocationElsweide & Kenniscampus Arnhem-Velp
FocusTransformer houses
TeamTeam size & programmes

The challenge

More transformer houses are needed. Technical requirements are only part of it.

The energy transition needs more transformer houses in neighbourhoods. But location, appearance and residents' needs all influence how these structures fit into everyday life.

FyE's collaboration with Alliander connects students with energy challenges around Elsweide and the Kenniscampus Arnhem-Velp, including transformer houses. It opens up a broader question: could infrastructure offer something useful to the community as well?

Explore the possibilities

What could a transformer house give back?

Seating areas or e-bike charging points are examples of what students can explore. Switch them on to see the idea.

Seating and e-bike charging come from the case. Greenery and a resident-chosen design are further illustrative examples.

Why it matters

Both the electricity network and the people living alongside it.

Bringing different perspectives together creates space to consider the technical network and the community at the same time.

For companies, this is the opportunity FyE offers: a real challenge becomes a starting point for fresh thinking, practical learning and collaboration with emerging talent.

Case study · ElaadNL

A charging station needs replacing. Does its foundation have to go too?

PartnerElaadNL
Team6 HAN students
FocusStandardised foundation
DeliveredPrototypes & advice report

The challenge

Different manufacturers, different foundations.

Different charging-station manufacturers use different foundations. Replacing a station can therefore mean excavation, a new foundation and a renewed grid connection, even when the original foundation could last much longer.

ElaadNL wanted to develop a standardised foundation that could stay in place. With limited capacity to build a prototype internally, the organisation collaborated with six HAN students.

See the difference

Replacing a charging station.

STEP 1ExcavationDig out the old foundation
STEP 2New foundationPour or place a manufacturer-specific base
STEP 3New grid connectionRenew the connection to the grid
STEP 4New charging stationInstall the replacement

Today: four steps, even when the old foundation could last much longer.

What the students did

Materials, durability, safety and ease of installation.

The students explored materials, durability, safety and ease of installation. They worked with industry stakeholders, visited companies and developed prototypes supported by an advice report.

Their software-based predictions of long-term performance also helped ElaadNL improve the proposed standard.

Click a photo to enlarge.

The result

Extra development capacity, and insights the team hadn't considered.

The collaboration gave ElaadNL additional development capacity and new insights. Students gained hands-on industry experience while contributing to a design meant to reduce the work involved in future charging-station replacements.

It shows how a focused industry question can become a valuable collaboration between education and business.
